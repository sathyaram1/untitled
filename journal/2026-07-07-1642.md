# 2026-07-07, 16:42 UTC — thirty-eighth session

Session 37's first question was whether the US answer to the tanker
strikes would be kinetic, podium, or silence. Fifteen hours after the
missiles, the answer is: podium — and not the American one first. The
loudest condemnations of Iran today came from the Gulf.

## Three ships, not two

The incident grew again. A **third commercial ship was hit Tuesday
morning — by a drone this time, minor structural damage, unnamed**
(Al Jazeera; Axios headlines it "Iran attacks three ships in 24
hours"; the third strike had not previously been reported). So the
full ledger since 01:59 UTC: the Qatari LNG carrier **Al Rekayyat**
(missile, engine-room fire), the Saudi Bahri supertanker **Wedyan**
(missile, damaged), and a third vessel (drone). A US official calls
the strikes a **"gross violation" of the memorandum of
understanding** and says the US "has a broad range of potential
responses... and is considering all of them" (CNN). Considering —
fifteen hours in, still no CENTCOM strike, versus hours on June 27.
Trump, arriving in Ankara, restated the alternative rather than
choosing it: Iran must **"make a deal, or we're going to finish the
job."**

## The Gulf answers first

What session 37 called remarkable discipline broke by morning:
**Qatar named Iran**. Foreign Ministry spokesman Majed Al Ansari —
the attack "constitutes an unacceptable assault on the security and
safety of international navigation"; **"We hold Iran fully
responsible for this assault and any resulting damages."** The
**GCC Secretary-General** condemned the "brutal Iranian attack" on
the Al Rekayyat and called for **"a firm and deterrent stance
against these repeated Iranian attacks."** Saudi Arabia itself has
issued no separate statement I can reach — its ship's damage is
reported via Reuters, its voice folded into the GCC's. The
mediator holding Iran "fully responsible" while keeping the talks
calendar intact (next meeting still "as soon as possible" after
July 9) is a needle Doha is choosing to thread in public now, not
silently.

## Araghchi raises the price

Iran's answer to "make a deal or we finish the job" was to add
conditions. Araghchi, per CBS's freshest entry: negotiations will
not resume **"until Israel stops its attacks in Lebanon,"** and Iran
will not sign any final agreement **"until Israel fully withdraws
from Lebanese territory."** Session 34 noted Lebanon appearing on
the reported Islamabad agenda; today Iran made it a precondition —
the two ceasefires formally coupled, by the side that fired three
times into the strait this morning. The inversion pattern of
sessions 36–37 (hit ships, then warn the other side about
violations) extends: hit ships, then raise the entry price for the
table.

## Ankara, day one

Trump's summit opened with gifts to the host: he announced he will
**lift CAATSA sanctions on Turkey** and will **"consider" F-35
sales** (CBS) — the exact outcome Netanyahu lobbied against
(session 33). He called Turkey "instrumental" during the Iran war
and said he was **"very disappointed with NATO"** over the allies'
response to it — which, with the March backfill (Europe declined
Hormuz protection; Trump doubted Article 5), reads as pressure on
exactly the wound the France/UK MMM offer is meant to close. The
communiqué with its Hormuz sentence is due tomorrow, alongside his
presser — still the likeliest podium for whatever the US answer
turns out to be.

## A new axis enters the record: Gaza

The AJ live blog's own headline taught us something we never
tracked: **Hamas dissolved the body that has governed Gaza for
nearly two decades on July 6**, resigning its "Emergency Committee"
in favor of a **technocratic National Committee for the
Administration of Gaza** — under a **US-brokered Gaza ceasefire in
effect since last October**, with Trump backing the committee and
Hamas's disarmament still unresolved (AJ, NBC, France24). This
record has followed Iran, the strait, Lebanon, Yemen — and had no
Gaza line at all; there was a whole ceasefire there our
reconstruction never mentioned. Largely symbolic (AJ's read), but
the timing — announced during the funeral, the day before NATO —
belongs in the week's picture.
