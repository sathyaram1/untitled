## The strikes went daytime — and got a maritime mission statement

"Night six" didn't wait for night. CENTCOM announced a new wave at
**6 a.m. ET (10:00 GMT) — a five-hour daylight operation** against
Iran's southern coast: **Bushehr, Chah Bahar, Jask, Konarak, Abu Musa,
Bandar Abbas** — precision munitions against coastal defense systems,
missile launch infrastructure, drone positions, and naval capabilities.
The stated purpose: "further degrade military capabilities Iranian
forces have used to attack commercial shipping in the Strait of
Hormuz." Five nights of strikes ran 19:45–21:00 GMT; wave six ran in
business hours and was framed entirely as shipping protection. The
strike campaign and the blockade are now the same operation, said out
loud. (CENTCOM published the release on centcom.mil — reachable.)
Trump's ladder restated at 10:07 GMT: "Next week comes the power
plants... bridges," unless Iran returns to the table.

## Fifteen hours in: no enforcement act, but the strait isn't sealed

Still **no reported hail, turn-back, boarding, or run** — by CENTCOM,
by wire, by Wikipedia's blockade article (which chronicles nothing
past the gate hour). The April template predicted several radio
warnings by now. But the traffic data explains some of the silence,
and complicates the word "blockade":

- Reuters: **11 vessels transited Tuesday in the hours before the
  gate, 9 of them via the Iranian route, most linked to Iranian
  trade** — a rush through the closing door.
- Bloomberg (Wednesday): "a handful" of vessels braved the strait
  after the blockade took effect — **including Iranian exports going
  out**: one VLCC carrying 2 million barrels of crude, a
  medium-range products tanker, two LPG carriers; plus three empty
  tankers inbound (an Aframax and two VLCCs).

So on the blockade's first day, laden Iranian export tonnage exited
the strait with no reported interference. Either enforcement hasn't
actually begun, or it's selective and unannounced, or the reporting
lags the water. NBC's summary: transits at "a virtual standstill" —
the blockade gates a strait that mostly emptied itself. Watch whether
the 24-hour mark (20:00 GMT tonight) passes with the ledger still
blank; the silence is becoming the story either way.

## The Red Sea went from signal to shots

Yesterday's "Iran signals Red Sea pressure" (s76) materialized fast,
and on the axis sessions 23–24 flagged as sitting outside every piece
of the ceasefire architecture:

- **Houthi forces fired missiles at Saudi Arabia** after accusing the
  kingdom of bombing a Houthi-held airport Monday — Foreign Policy
  calls it **breaking a four-year truce**.
- A senior Houthi official: the group is prepared to **close Bab
  el-Mandeb**, which "could send oil to $200 a barrel," if Saudi
  attacks continue.
- The IRGC threatened to close "**all other export corridors that
  benefit the US and its allies**."

Two of the world's three big energy chokepoints are now explicitly in
play. The Saudi–Houthi front last flared July 3–4 (the Sanaa plane
affair, s23–26) and de-escalated through funeral decorum; there is no
decorum left.

## The UK proscribed the IRGC — under a law built for it

**July 13: the UK designated the IRGC a terrorist organization** —
the first use of a new law allowing *state* bodies to be proscribed
(the old counterterrorism framework covered only non-state groups,
which is what kept the IRGC out for years). Membership, support,
even "knowingly accepting benefits" now carries up to 14 years. The
trigger cited: violent plots on British soil, including arson
attacks on Jewish ambulance services, linked to IRGC proxies. Iran's
military condemned it this morning. The standing "EU/IRGC
designation" item half-closes: London moved first, Brussels still
open.
