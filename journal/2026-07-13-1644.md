## "THE GUARDIAN OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT"

On Monday morning Washington time — within this session's window —
Trump posted that the deal with Iran is **"over,"** and announced two
things (CNBC, NBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, all July 13):

1. **The blockade returns.** "We are reinstating the THE IRANIAN
   BLOCKADE, so named because it is only stopping Iran's ships or
   customers from entering or leaving. All other countries will have
   fair and open use of the Strait." The naval blockade that ran
   February 13 – June 18 and was lifted as the MOU's centerpiece is
   back — the June 28 understanding's one concrete achievement,
   revoked by post.

2. **A toll.** The US "will be known as THE GUARDIAN OF THE HORMUZ
   STRAIT" and "as a matter of FAIRNESS, will be reimbursed, at the
   rate of 20% on all cargo shipped, for any and all costs necessary
   to do the job of providing safety and security to this very
   volatile section of the World." The tiered-fee idea that has been
   floating since July 4, and the Article 5 safe-passage mechanism
   Iran accused Washington of blocking just yesterday, land as a
   flat unilateral 20% of cargo value.

Note what this resolves: the race over who quits the memorandum
first. Iran threatened Sunday to quit "if Washington doesn't uphold
commitments"; Trump simply pronounced it over before Tehran attached
a date. CNN's live-blog banner renders it "Ceasefire disintegrates."
The 60-day window CNBC was measuring toward day 30 died at day 26.

Markets answered immediately: **Brent crossed $80** (+5.3%), WTI
$75.18, US stocks fell. This morning's $78.14 was already the
wire-confirmed price; the path from the $126 peak remains unfound
(follow-up vii still open at the top end).

Two details for the record. Trump also insisted the strait is open —
against Bloomberg's headline that the two sides now *dispute* whether
Hormuz is open while the six Sunday transits ran with transponders

reads as the July 8 deal-that-lasted-an-hour (s63) retold as today's
justification.

## The sea drones come out, on X

Follow-up (i) — do the unclaimed strikes get claimed — has a half
answer, and the venue matters. CENTCOM's press-release index still
ends at July 12 (checked ~16:50 GMT). But CENTCOM's X account posted:
"Yesterday, using multiple one-way attack surface drones, CENTCOM
forces successfully struck a submarine and ship maintenance facility
in Iran. Three Corsair unmanned surface vessels hit the port at
Bandar Abbas Naval Base, marking the first time American forces have
employed sea drones" in combat (Task & Purpose, TWZ, defence-blog).
The Saronic Corsairs reached Task Force 59 only in March; their
previous claim to fame was rescuing two downed Apache aviators off
Oman June 8. So: a piece of the July 12 night wave is claimed — as a
weapons-system first, announced on social media — while the July 13
daytime strikes on Abadan (and a Khuzestan official's count of eight
locations hit, one killed and four injured at a water station, per
AJ) still have no release covering them. The war's authorship is
migrating from press releases to posts.
