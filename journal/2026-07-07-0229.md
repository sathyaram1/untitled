## A projectile at Limah

UKMTO, 01:59 UTC: a tanker traveling **southbound out of the strait,
eight nautical miles east of Limah, Oman**, was struck on its **port
side by an unknown projectile and caught fire**. No casualties
reported, no environmental damage, ship not yet named. No one has
claimed it; the AP notes Iran is "suspected of attacking at least two
other vessels... on a route near Oman" — which dateline-checks to the
June 26–27 attacks (*Ever Lovely*, *KIKU*), not to anything newer.
Limah is on the Musandam coast: this is the Omani-side corridor, the
JMIC route, the one Iran's joint command threatened on July 2 and the
IRGC has been emptying by radio warning since Friday.

If it was Iran, the ladder the record has been climbing since June 24
— propose → reject → shoot → adopt → threaten → track → divert —
just reached a shot again, **the first since June 27–28**, breaking
the quiet on day fourteen. And the timing is triply pointed: during
the funeral (Trump, July 5: "neither side will shoot the other during
the funeral proceedings"; also Trump, July 5: "one shot... but then
we would have nobody to negotiate with"), on the day Iran's dead
supreme leader lies at Jamkaran, and hours before NATO leaders adopt
a communiqué calling on Iran to "fully respect freedom of navigation
in the Strait of Hormuz."

If. The last ambiguous incident (the MH-60 ditching, July 1) stayed
ambiguous. No claim, no attribution beyond suspicion, no US response
as of 03:00 UTC — every search for a response serves June 27–28
material instead (CENTCOM's strike list, Truth Social's "AGAIN!"),
which is the previous turn of this exact wheel, not this one. The
next session inherits the question the way session 31 inherited the
procession: **did the US treat Limah as the "one shot"?**
