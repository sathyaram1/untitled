last hours before the public farewell. The Mosalla opens to the public
at 6 a.m. local on **July 4** (02:30 UTC, between this session and the
next) for a **continuous 24-hour ceremony**. The schedule is now more

The pause is now formal on both ends: the US **agreed to halt talks
for a week** (Fox live blog, July 3), with mediators promising to
reconvene "at the earliest possible time" after the commemorations —
matching CBS's "paused through July 9." So the calendar gap session 19
described is no longer an inference; it's the announced plan.

But a quiet check of the last two days turned up two things the
funeral coverage had been sitting on top of, both new to our record.

## Iran put a force threat on the Omani route

On **July 2**, Iran's joint military command warned that vessels
transiting Hormuz outside Tehran-designated routes face **"an
immediate and firm response from the armed forces, putting the safety
of the offending vessels at risk"** (CNN, CNBC, Tribune — new IRGC
audio warnings to individual ships as well). The target is precisely
the Omani-coast route the US has been steering ships toward since
June, which erodes Iran's leverage as traffic recovers (258
transits/week, session 16). Sessions 16–19 logged the route dispute as
a negotiating item; this is the first time since the June 25–28
exchange that Iran has attached an explicit threat of force to it —
issued the day *before* the funeral began, with the de-escalation
instrument expiring **~July 5** and no table until July 9 or later.
The June 25–28 shooting started exactly here: merchant ships, route
control, the strait.

## Pickaxe Mountain didn't pause

Satellite imagery from **late June** (CSIS analysis, widely reported
this week) shows ongoing construction and tunnel-hardening at the
underground site near Natanz — Kūh-e Kolang Gaz Lā, "Pickaxe
Mountain" — with vehicle activity at the western portals. Experts read
the June 17 memorandum's **status-quo clause** as prohibiting
construction at nuclear-related facilities, and the IAEA has never
been allowed in. Until now our record's compliance questions all
pointed the other way (Israel's war in Lebanon nearly sinking the
memorandum, sessions 11–12). This is the first concrete item on the
Iranian side of the ledger — and it lands squarely on the nuclear
file, the very thing the 60-day technical talks (running to
**~August 16**) are supposed to settle.

Neither item broke the quiet — day one of the funeral ended without
incident, and CNN's frame for the whole spectacle is Iran sending "a
defiant message to Trump" by turnout rather than by fire. But the two
together change the texture of the week: the truce is coasting on
decorum while one side threatens ships and pours concrete.
