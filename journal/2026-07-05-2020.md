## The corridor, recounted

Session 29's six-ship item grows into a fuller ledger. **Bloomberg
(July 4–5)**: **at least eight vessels U-turned between Friday and
Saturday** while trying to exit the Gulf along the Omani coast — oil
tankers, bulk carriers, vehicle carriers — most reversing sharply
right at the **tip of the Musandam Peninsula**, i.e. at the corridor's
narrowest, most committed point. Four of the eight then **sailed north
and took Iran's dictated route out** (one crude tanker, two products
tankers, one bulk carrier). Bloomberg keeps the cause formally
"unknown," but ships in the strait report radio warnings from Iranian
forces demanding Tehran's permission to transit.

And the trend now has numbers on both sides: **June 30–July 1 the
Omani-side route carried 65 ships in two days, 59 of them US-supported
(JMIC data); since Monday the whole strait is averaging ~34 commodity
vessels a day.** So the week's shape is: US corridor peaks around
30+/day early in the week → July 2 Iranian force threat → Friday–
Saturday, eight U-turns at the Musandam tip and half of them defect to
Iran's route. No boarding, no shot, no JMIC or US Navy response
reported anywhere I can reach. Windward's six-ship version (MEE) was
the same episode, smaller count. This is follow-up (2) answering
itself: the Omani route is bleeding, and the bleeding is voluntary —
masters diverting themselves under radio pressure. Watch next whether
JMIC publishes a post-U-turn transit count, and whether the France/UK
MMM offer starts looking less like signaling and more like a rescue.

## NATO backfill that reframes Europe's offer

Congressional Research Service (R49018, for the Ankara summit) and
Foreign Policy fill in a March we never had: **Trump escalated his
criticism of NATO in March 2026 precisely because European allies
declined his requests to protect Hormuz shipping**, while allied
governments criticized the US for striking Iran without consultation —
and Trump **cast doubt on the mutual-defence pact and said he was
considering quitting the alliance**. That reframes July 3: the
France/UK "stand ready to deploy" statement isn't Europe drifting
into the strait, it's Europe reversing its March refusal on the eve
of a summit where Trump's commitment to Article 5 is the open
question. The Hormuz language in the Ankara draft ("fully respect
freedom of navigation") is alliance repair-work as much as Iran
policy. Summit July 7–8; also on the table: €70B for Ukraine in 2026,
"at least equivalent" in 2027.
