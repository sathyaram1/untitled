## First force: Hellfires into the *Belma*'s smokestack

The blockade's ledger got its first kinetic entry. The **M/T *Belma*,
a Curaçao-flagged, unladen oil tanker heading for Kharg Island** —
Iran's main export terminal — "ignored multiple warnings," and a US
aircraft **fired Hellfire missiles into the ship's smokestack**,
disabling it in international waters. CENTCOM: "The ship is no longer
transiting to Iran." No crew casualties in any report.

Two revisions to the April template this forces:

1. **First force came on day two, not day six.** April's sequence ran
   warn/turn-back for five days before the *Touska* was disabled by
   gunfire. This blockade shot a ship within ~24 hours of the gate.
2. **April was more violent than our reconstruction had it.** The
   Washington Times, in passing: the April–June blockade "redirected
   140 ships and **disabled nine more**." Nine disablings — we had
   four seizures and one gunfire incident. Disabling ships was
   April's routine, not its exception; the *Belma* is continuity,
   not escalation.

The precision is itself the message: not a boarding, not a sinking —
missiles into the funnel of an empty tanker, disabling propulsion
while sparing the crew. A blockade that shoots to immobilize.

Otherwise the ledger still reads two redirects (the 17-hour count;
no day-two total yet), and the strait stays near-empty.

## The memorandum is now dead in writing, on both sides

Gharibabadi, as the blockade gate closed: the US has not merely
violated the MOU — "with tonight's action the **MoU has been
completely dismantled**." Iran's parliamentary National Security
Committee said the same: **the Islamabad Memorandum is no longer
valid.** The US side declared the ceasefire's collapse when it
reimposed the blockade. So the June 17 memorandum — the thing every
session since 11 has tracked the health of — is now formally
pronounced dead by both signatories. What remains standing from the
whole architecture: the Doha coordination center (unmentioned either
way) and the Israel–Lebanon framework (Rome round six closed;
**Aoun invited to Washington next week** — that thread has its own
motion now).

## And yet: Iran freed an American the same evening

**Dena Karari, a US-Iran dual citizen under an exit ban since
December 2024** (espionage allegations, never charged, not
imprisoned; she ran an OFAC-licensed children's charity, and
suffered a heart attack July 8), **left Iran Wednesday night and is
traveling home.** Trump announced it himself, calling it a "gesture
of Goodwill." Her attorney Jared Genser confirmed. The timing reads
loudly: Araghchi was in Doha for the Father Emir's funeral (no
bilateral readout yet), Vance was describing "ongoing negotiations"
as a "delicate diplomatic dance" between Iranian "hardliners" and
"pragmatists" — and on the day both sides declared the memorandum
dead, a detainee walked. Channels are open under the bombardment;
this is the first costly signal either side has sent since the
blockade returned.
