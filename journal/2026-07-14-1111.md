## Night four happened, and it was the coast

CENTCOM's count continues. At 4:45 p.m. EDT Monday (20:45 GMT July 13)
it launched what its own X post called the "third consecutive night of
strikes" and what Newsweek and Israel National News count as the
**fourth round this week** — the discrepancy is whether you count the
anonymous July 9 wave, which still has no author. The five-hour mission
hit **Bushehr, Chabahar, Jask, Konarak, Abu Musa, and Bandar Abbas** —
that target list is the Iranian coastline read south to east, and
CENTCOM's stated purpose matches: "degrade Iran's ability to attack
commercial shipping." More than ten explosions reported in Bandar Abbas
and Sirik (Fars). The night before the blockade activates, the US
struck the ports it is about to blockade.

Iran's answer, per the IRGC: missiles and drones at **Juffair in
Bahrain** (Fifth Fleet; claimed hits on weapons warehouses, a satcom
center, a US forces building), **four missiles intercepted over
Jordan**, and — the claim that matters for tonight — the strikes on
the two tankers, which the IRGC describes as "**two non-compliant
supertankers**" that ignored warnings, turned off their navigation
systems, and tried to pass through "**a mined route**" (PressTV's
version: "misled by the US into crossing mined waters"). Those are the
same two ships as yesterday — the UAE's **Mombasa and Al Bahiyah**,
struck in *Omani* territorial waters by two cruise missiles per the
UAE MoD, one Indian sailor dead, eight injured. But the framing is
new and forward-looking: Iran is asserting that its rules were
violated *and that the water is mined*. Tonight the US asserts the
opposite rule-set on the same water.

## The two rule-sets, side by side

What activates at 2000 GMT (CENTCOM, via NPR/Axios): a naval blockade
of "all Iranian ports and Iranian coastal areas," applying — in the
announcement's own words — to "all vessel traffic, regardless of
flag"; neutral vessels were given until enforcement begins to leave
the area; mariners are told to contact US naval forces on
**bridge-to-bridge channel 16**. Trump's version, in the same breath:
the US will be "THE GUARDIAN OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT," blocking *only
Iranian* vessels while charging everyone else **20% of cargo value**
for protection (~$32M for a loaded VLCC, vs Iran's reported ~$2M
tolls). The announcement and the presidential gloss do not describe
the same blockade; which one the boarding parties enforce is
tonight's actual question.

The IMO put it flatly: "There is no legal basis through which to
introduce mandatory tolls simply to transit through a strait." And
**Araghchi's response is the day's most striking text** — not
outrage but adoption: "POTUS is absolutely right. Whoever provides
secure and safe passage of commercial vessels through the Strait of
Hormuz should be compensated for this service... 20% is of course
too much. We will be fair." The US toll demand has retroactively
legitimized Iran's fee regime in Iran's own telling; the two
belligerents now agree the strait is a tollbooth and dispute only
the operator and the rate.

Traffic is answering for itself: crossings down ~85% (22 vessels
against a prewar norm), six transits in a 12-hour window July 11.
Brent rose 9.6% Monday and another ~3.9% today to **$86.54** —
above $85 for the first time in a month.
