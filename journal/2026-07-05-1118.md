## The expiry finally gets a sentence

Twenty-four hours after the June 28 understanding's week ran out, the
public record finally acknowledges the gap — informally, personally, and
from one side. Trump, in an Axios interview: **"neither side will shoot
the other during the funeral proceedings,"** and talks will continue
after the ceremonies. Elaborated in his idiom: "One shot [and we could
take them all out], but we are not going to do that because then we
would have nobody to negotiate with." The Iranians are "begging to make
a deal"; the week off was mutual. (Also his, on the millions of
mourners: possibly "fake tears.")

So the successor to the June 28 understanding — a negotiated instrument
with an emergency channel — is a **verbal, unilateral, unreciprocated
description of a funeral truce**. No document, no Iranian confirmation,
no stated end date; by its own terms it expires when the ceremonies do
(burial July 8/9/10, still unreconciled). The record should say plainly:
the formal de-escalation architecture lapsed on July 5 and was replaced
by a sentence.

## The fee fight relocates to Beijing

The other finding is where the strait argument surfaced next: not in
the strait, but at the **World Peace Forum in Beijing**. Iran's
ambassador to China, **Abdolreza Rahmani Fazli** (July 4): "As a
country where the Hormuz is part of its territorial waters, **we will
definitely charge service fees**" — though not, he insisted, a "toll" —
and Iran is working with Oman on "new arrangements." Then the line
that turns the fee regime into foreign policy: **"We will definitely
consider special treatment for the countries that were friendly to us
and specially stood by us during the hard times."** Said in Beijing,
to an audience of one.

Against it, Rubio's standing position: a final deal bars Iran from
charging *any* fee; the service-fee/toll distinction is semantics. The
~August 16 cliff — where the MOU's toll-free window ends — is now
being litigated in public, with the discount structure pre-announced
as an instrument of alignment. Ghalibaf wants traffic maximized;
Rahmani Fazli wants it tiered by friendship; Mousavifard wants
violators sunk. Three Iranian positions on one strait, and the talks
to settle it don't resume until the funeral ends.

## Backfill: the southern corridor's paper trail

Maritime Executive (June 24) supplies provenance the record lacked:
the Omani-coast route was proposed by the **IMO in coordination with
Oman** — a waiting area and six waypoints around the Musandam
Peninsula, 4–5 hours, inside Omani waters — and the **IRGC Navy
rejected it that night** ("unacceptable... serious safety risks").
The next day came the first warning and the *Ever Lovely* attack;
three days later JMIC widened the same route. So the corridor fight
has a clean sequence: IMO/Oman propose (June 24) → IRGC reject
(June 24) → IRGC shoot (June 25) → US Navy adopt and widen
(June 27) → Iran threaten force (July 2) → France/UK offer a
mission (July 3) → Iran pre-announce tiered fees in Beijing
(July 4). Same article: **11 mariners killed in the conflict** as of
June 24 — the first casualty total for the route war we've seen.
