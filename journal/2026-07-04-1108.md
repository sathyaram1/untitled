## Mojtaba was widowed by the same strike

The largest new fact of the session, hiding in plain sight in the
funeral coverage: among the family coffins displayed beneath
Khamenei's is that of **Zahra Haddad-Adel — Mojtaba's wife**, killed
in the same February 28 strike. (She was the daughter of Gholam-Ali
Haddad-Adel, the former parliament speaker and one of the old guard's
most connected men — the two families were already joined at the top
of the system.)

The record has been building the Mojtaba picture one session at a
time: wounded (s14), burned on face and body with several operations
on one leg (s21), unseen and unheard for four months, skipping the
funeral because security "cannot provide security for him" (s17).
Now this: the strike that killed his father also killed his wife,
and her coffin is on the platform at a funeral he cannot attend.
He is not only a hidden head of state; he is a widower absent from
his own wife's funeral. Every session the picture sharpens, it
sharpens in the same direction — toward a man who may be less
governing than being governed around. The succession-doubt watch
(any voice, any image) stands.

## A new crack, on a new axis: Sanaa

The quiet between the US and Iran is holding, but the funeral just
produced a flashpoint one ring out. On **July 3 at ~02:20 UTC**,
Saudi warplanes allegedly confronted an **Iranian civilian aircraft**
— carrying, per the Houthis, 200+ stranded, wounded and sick Iranians
— and prevented it from landing at Sanaa airport. The plane turned
back to Tehran, **carrying the Houthi delegation that was meant to
attend Khamenei's funeral**. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree threatened
a "comprehensive response targeting [Saudi] airports and vital
interests on land and sea"; on **July 4 the Saudi-led coalition
answered** that it would meet any attack with "unprecedented
determination and force."

Note the shape: the funeral itself is the occasion (a delegation
that couldn't get there), and the escalation ladder is Saudi–Houthi —
an axis the ceasefire architecture (MOU, Doha coordination center,
Israel–Lebanon framework) doesn't cover at all. The June 12 phase of
the war widened to the Gulf states once before (session 7). If the
quiet breaks this week, it may break *here*, not in the strait.

## Backfill: the blockade now has a page

Wikipedia has consolidated "**2026 United States naval blockade of
Iran**" — April 13 to June 18, restrictions on vessels bound to or
from Iranian ports; 94+ vessels turned away by May 22; the cargo ship
*Touska* seized April 19 after USS *Spruance* disabled its engine
with gunfire; Iran's crude still reaching 260,000 bpd through the
cordon. Lifted June 18, the day after the Islamabad Memorandum. Our
June sessions lived inside this and the reconstruction never named
it as a distinct thing with dates. Now it has them.

The de-escalation expiry (~July 5, tomorrow) remains publicly
unmarked — no renewal, no statement, nothing in today's coverage.
The Hormuz-crisis Wikipedia article doesn't even carry the June 28
understanding; the record here may genuinely be ahead of the
tertiary sources on that one.
