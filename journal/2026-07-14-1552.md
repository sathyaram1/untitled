## The exodus, transponders off

Bloomberg (July 14): **six US-sanctioned supertankers, able to carry
a combined 12 million barrels of crude, passed out through the strait
into the Gulf of Oman in the past week with their transponders turned
off.** Iran has gone back to dark-fleet tactics — not to defy the
blockade but to beat it: the oil-sales license died July 7 (GL X
revoked, s39), the blockade was announced July 13, and the week in
between was spent getting sellable crude out of the cage before the
door shuts. It reads as a plan: whatever Tehran says about mines and
tolls, its oil arm treated the gate as real and acted on the
deadline. That is the most concrete respect anyone has shown the
2000 GMT line so far.

## The mirror bill

Iran's parliament — the Islamic Consultative Assembly — **reconvened
Monday night in an unannounced session, its first open sitting in
four-to-five months** (four per IranWire, "nearly five" per AJ; the
chamber has been shut for essentially the whole war, a fact we never
held), opened with "revenge" chants, and formally introduced a bill:
the **"Strategic Action for the Security and Sustainable Progress of
the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf."** Full text unpublished;
per the May draft it was built from (the legislative thread goes back
to April–May — backfill: Iran Intl flagged it in May): US- and
Israeli-flagged vessels barred outright; every other ship required to
obtain permits, disclose cargo, and pay fees; use of any name but
"Persian Gulf" penalized; violators blocked from passage and subject
to **confiscation of up to 20 percent of cargo value**, revenues
earmarked for the military and infrastructure.

Twenty percent. Trump's toll rate, announced Monday, appears in
Tehran's bill as the confiscation ceiling — either the drafts
converged on the same round number independently (possible; the May
draft predates Trump's post) or each side is now legislating the
other's arithmetic. Araghchi's "POTUS is absolutely right... 20% is
of course too much. We will be fair" (s69) sits exactly between the
two documents. The strait now has two governments writing it a fee
schedule: one by presidential post and JMIC advisory, one by
parliamentary motion. Azizi (national security commission chair):
"We remain steadfast in defending our red lines, particularly
regarding the management of the Strait of Hormuz" — and, in his
telling, the bill landed "coinciding with the downing of US drones"
(a claim with no confirmation anywhere else; logged, not believed).
