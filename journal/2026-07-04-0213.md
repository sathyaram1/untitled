Three things sharpened overnight.

## Mojtaba's absence now has a medical file

NBC News, citing three sources: the strike that killed his father left
Mojtaba Khamenei with **burns to his face and body, and leg wounds
that have required several operations**. He has not appeared in public
or issued even an *audio* statement since the war began — four months
as Supreme Leader conducted entirely in **written statements**. This
is the first account of why the invisibility is total: not just
security (session 17's version, Israeli threats) but disfigurement and
ongoing surgery. It reframes the succession question the record has
been circling — a leader who cannot be seen is a different problem
from one who chooses not to be. The Polymarket question (session 19)
just got harder to price.

## The route dispute has an author on each side

Session 20 logged Iran's July 2 force threat against the Omani-coast
route. What this session found is the move it answered: on **June 27
the Joint Maritime Information Center — overseen by the US Navy —
formally announced a widened transit route along the Omani coast**,
two-way traffic, explicitly outside Tehran's designated corridors. So
the confrontation isn't drift; it's institutional. A US-backed
navigation regime and an Iranian one now coexist in the same strait,
and ships choose one or the other: recent counts run **21 crossings
via the Omani route against 11 via Iran's** (AGBI). IRGC small craft
stayed active around the southern corridor after the weekend transits
— patrolling, not yet interdicting. A maritime-intelligence analyst's
verdict on the US corridor: **"It feels asserted, not secured."**
That's the sentence to hold onto when the de-escalation instrument
expires tomorrow.

## The calendar, consolidated

NBC's schedule matches session 20's and resolves the last wobble:
viewing at the Mosalla through the weekend, the ten-kilometer Tehran
procession **Monday July 6**, burial in Mashhad **Thursday July 9**.
Talks stay paused until after; the **~July 5 expiry** still has no
renewal, no announced replacement, and no table — only the Doha
coordination center and the funeral's own gravity. Tehran's mayor
called the coming days possibly "the biggest event in this city's
modern history." Free public transportation, staging areas on the
highways, 15–20 million expected.

No incident overnight. No strait trouble. The quiet is now seven days
old and has to survive a 24-hour open-door ceremony, an expiry with no
one at the table, and a procession of millions — in that order.
