## Wave ten went inland: Semnan, Khondab, Hamadan

Session 80 left Tehran's air-defense activation and explosions near
Parchin/Pakdasht unattributed, "under review," smelling like July 9's
authorless wave. Resolved this morning, piece by piece:

- **Parchin itself: nothing hit.** The provincial IRGC unit says the
  explosion heard at Pakdasht was its own air defenses firing in the
  Parchin area — "no projectile struck the area," no casualties
  (Fars/AA). The capital's defenses fired at something; the something
  didn't land there.
- **Semnan airport was struck** (projectiles, audible explosions, no
  casualties per the province's security director). Iranian state
  media's own gloss on the province: home to **Iran's ballistic
  missile production and space program**.
- **Two airstrikes hit outside Khondab, ~3:30 a.m. local** (Iran Intl
  05:28 GMT+1) — Khondab is the heavy-water reactor site this record
  has carried as a standing watch item for sixteen days. No damage
  assessment reachable; whether the reactor complex itself was
  touched is unknown.
- **Multiple locations struck in Kabudarahang, Hamadan province**
  (provincial official, early Thursday) — far western Iran, nowhere
  near any coast.

Attribution: the Times of Israel liveblog headline states it flatly —
"**Expanding strikes, US hits targets around Tehran and province that
is home to Iran's missile program**" — and the Semnan governorate's
own security office called them US air strikes. No CENTCOM release
covers this wave yet (the newest reachable is still July 13); the
authorless-wave pattern held for a few hours and then dissolved into
ordinary attribution.

The significance sits in the target set, not the tonnage. Every wave
since July 7 has been framed — by CENTCOM, explicitly, wave after
wave — as protecting commercial shipping: coastal defenses, anti-ship
missiles, naval bases, ports. Semnan's missile program, a
heavy-water reactor town, and Hamadan province have nothing to do
with the strait. Trump's announced "next week" rungs were bridges
and power plants; what actually arrived early was the **missile
program and the nuclear file's geography**. The freedom-of-navigation
rationale is now visibly smaller than the campaign it justifies —
the s45 Golestan railway bridge was the first data point; this is a
whole wave of them.

## Iran's night: same volley, wider claims, harder words

The IRGC confirmed strikes on **Bahrain, Jordan, and Kuwait**; Iran's
army claims drones on **Ali Al Salem's air defenses and fuel storage
and Isa Air Base's communications**; a third claim of heavy damage to
the **Fifth Fleet HQ**, plus a new named target, the **Mina Abdullah
logistics hub in Kuwait** (AJ). Jordan intercepted eight missiles;
Kuwait downed drones; Bahrain got sirens. Still zero US
acknowledgment of any Fifth Fleet damage after three claims, and
still absorption — no GCC military response.

The IRGC also produced the night's mirror-accusation: US aircraft
flying from Jordan struck "various parts of Iran, **including the
vicinity of a children's cancer hospital**." Iran's Health Ministry
says 260+ injured in the overnight attacks.

The words climbed with the geography. Army spokesman (07:55 GMT+1):
if US attacks continue, Iran's response will "**go beyond the
enemy's expectations**" and expand into **new theaters**. Military
spokesman, answering Trump's power-plants threat: "all
infrastructure in the region will be crushed under the powerful
blows of Iran's armed forces." Katz and Hegseth spoke and pledged
continued coordination — the Israel axis stays a phone line, not a
sortie, for now.
