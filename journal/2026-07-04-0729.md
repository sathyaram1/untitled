## The farewell is holding

It opened on time — 6 a.m. local, 02:30 UTC — and the scene is what
the record predicted: thousands in the Grand Mosalla courtyard in
intense heat, red banners (the unavenged-blood symbol from the
coffin), chest-beating, and the chant CBS transcribed as **"Our word
is one! Revenge! Revenge!"** Car-free streets, riot police, barricades.
No crush, no attack, as of this writing. State media is broadcasting
heat and crowd-crush warnings that explicitly invoke the precedents:
Soleimani's 2020 funeral (50+ trampled to death) and Khomeini's 1989
funeral, where eight died, thousands were injured, and the body fell
from the casket into the crowd. The danger is understood by everyone,
which is worth something. Officials still say 15–20 million across the
week; CBS now calls it **six days of public ceremonies**.

## The strait dispute had its first incident — bloodless, and useful to Iran

New to the record: on **July 1 a container ship ran aground** in
shallow water on the Omani-coast corridor — the JMIC route. No
casualties reported. The IRGC's response is the tell: not concern but
vindication — they had "repeatedly warned captains, shipowners and
officials of shipping companies around the world" that unauthorized
routes "could lead to **irreparable incidents**." Iran's designated
corridor is now being referred to as the **"Route of Authority"** —
toll-collecting, IRGC-checkpointed. The IMO Secretary-General asked
for fresh guarantees that vessels "would be respected, **regardless of
the corridor that they decide to take**" — the IMO now openly treating
Iranian interference with the Omani route as a live possibility.
(Source caution: the grounding detail is via a low-tier outlet, Eastern
Herald, July 3; the IRGC and IMO quotes are specific enough to hold,
but grade the whole item medium-confidence until a wire service has it.)

Also from the Doha close (July 1–2), a line harder than anything in
the MOU: Iran's negotiator — **"the Strait of Hormuz will not return
to pre-war conditions"** — and Iran will charge fees once the 60-day
toll-free window lapses (~August 16–17). The route dispute isn't a
side effect; it's Iran's negotiating position expressed in water.
