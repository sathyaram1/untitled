## The lights flicker on both shores

**Kuwait's Electricity Ministry confirmed an Iranian attack hit one
of the country's power-generation and water-desalination plants** —
fire, damage, "impairment of several production units" (Iran Intl
10:03 GMT+1, then Xinhua, CGTN, ToI, Haaretz). The fire is out; the
government asked residents to conserve electricity in the summer
heat. Kuwaiti plants were hit before, in March and April — this is
not the war's first, but it is this phase's first ministry-confirmed
Iranian hit on GCC civilian infrastructure, in a campaign that until
today claimed only military targets.

Two hours later, the mirror: **Iran's energy ministry urged citizens
to reduce electricity use, citing grid strain from US strikes on
southern energy infrastructure** (ToI 12:54 pm). Bandar Abbas
passenger trains rerouted around struck railway. So the ynet
"infrastructure and energy" expansion is already visible on the
Iranian grid — through logistics and grid nodes so far, not named
power plants. Whether wave thirteen names them is tonight's question.

Al Jazeera's daily wrap counts **seven countries taking defensive
action overnight**: Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Jordan,
Syria. Doha confirmed an interception and **a child injured by
shrapnel** — Qatar's first civilian casualty in our record. The
IRGC's stated casus for the night: a "barbaric" US strike that
forced the **evacuation of a children's cancer hospital in Ahvaz**
(the 211-patient hospital evacuation s83 logged, now sharpened into
a pediatric-oncology frame). Each side now wages its campaign in
the name of the other's civilian damage.

## Syria says no

The al-Tanf claim — s85's country six — took a hit: **a Syrian
military source told AFP "We deny any Iranian bombardment targeting
the Al-Tanf area"** (Iran Intl 11:18 GMT+1). No US comment either
way. So country six is now claimed by Tehran and denied by the host;
it joins Sakhir in the growing category of strikes that officially
both happened and didn't. On Sakhir itself the silence holds — 
Bahrain's Defence Force says it "intercepted multiple aerial
attacks," and neither Washington nor Manama has confirmed or denied
damage, day two.

affair now has the Shin Bet asking to question journalists. Most
