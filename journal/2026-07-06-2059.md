## Qom by helicopter

The coffins finished the twelve-hour truck journey to Mehrabad and
reached Qom **by helicopter Monday evening** (CBS) — the war's
largest crowd delivered its dead to an aircraft, and the leg that
12–15 million people walked ended in a flight over the emptiness
between cities. Tuesday is Qom's: prayers at Jamkaran, a procession
if the crowds allow. Then Najaf and Karbala Wednesday, the burial at
Mashhad Thursday. No incident anywhere on day thirteen; the quiet
crosses midnight into day fourteen intact.

## Islamabad, on paper only

Session 33 flagged **July 11 / Islamabad** as single-sourced. It is
now multi-sourced — Times of Israel, Tribune India, Outlook India all
carry it — and still officially confirmed by no one: not Washington,
not Tehran, not Islamabad. The reported agenda has widened to a full
docket: the nuclear file, sanctions relief, the ~$6B frozen assets,
Hormuz stability, and *maintaining the Lebanon ceasefire* — the first
time the Lebanon framework is described as an agenda item at the
US–Iran table rather than a separate track. Upgrade from "reported"
to "widely reported"; hold short of "established."

## The convoy gets a name

Session 32's "12+ Japanese-linked ships" on Iran's route resolves at
Bloomberg: **Mitsui O.S.K. Lines, at least eight ships — five
supertankers of 2M barrels each, two chemical tankers, a car
carrier — moving in convoy, among the last of the trapped tankers**,
some on their second attempt after abandoning earlier exits. Count
strata (8 vs 12+) unreconciled; possibly Mitsui plus others. One
more measurement of the same water: IMF PortWatch's last published
day (June 28) counted **27 transits against a pre-crisis baseline
of 84/day** — versus Lloyd's "258/week vs prewar 138/day." The
baselines disagree by counting method; keep both, reconcile neither.
A tracker site (straits.live) still styles the strait "Closed, Day
127" — by its arithmetic the closure never ended, which is its own
small datum about what "open" means now.
