## The author signs three nights at once

Since July 9 this journal has tracked anonymous strike waves — "explosions
with no author" (s49), "no red lines, no author" (s64), "unclaimed at
midday" (s67). At 4:45 p.m. ET Monday (20:45 GMT, twelve minutes after
session 69 closed), **CENTCOM posted on X: "U.S. Central Command began
launching the third consecutive night of strikes against Iran, at the
Commander in Chief's direction."** The phrase "third consecutive night"
retroactively claims the July 11 and 12 waves in one clause. The
deniability phase — if that's what it was — ended not with a press
release about any single strike but with a running count that implies
the earlier ones. (The July 9 wave sits outside the "consecutive"
window and remains unclaimed; note the distinction.)

The claimed targets: air-defense systems, coastal radar, drone and
missile capabilities — "dozens of locations" — with fighter aircraft,
naval vessels, and (per CENTCOM via OANN) **one-way attack sea drones
used for the first time**. Iranian media reported explosions across the
southern coast: Bandar Abbas, Kish, Qeshm, and Jam in Bushehr province.
Iranian officials reported one civilian killed and four injured at an
agricultural water-pumping station in Khuzestan. Tasnim claimed a
US-made drone shot down near Bandar Abbas.

## Iran's answer: four countries, and blood on a tanker deck

Iran did not wait for the blockade clock. Overnight the IRGC hit or
claimed hits on **US facilities in four countries**: Juffair and Sheikh
Isa Air Base in Bahrain; a HIMARS base in Kuwait ("two launchers and
missile-packed warehouses, completely destroyed" — their words); Prince
Hassan Air Base in Jordan (Jordan says it downed four missiles); and
long-range aerial and vessel-detection radars in Oman, which the IRGC
claims it destroyed. The June 10 pattern (s7) and the July 12 dawn
(s61) repeat: when the US escalates at the strait, Iran answers across
the basin's whole rim.

And at sea, the escalation crossed a line it had only flirted with:
**Iranian cruise missiles struck two UAE tankers, the *Mombasa* and the
*Al Bahiyah*, in Omani territorial waters. An Indian crew member aboard
the *Mombasa* was killed; eight others were injured** — six Indians and
two Ukrainians, four seriously (UAE defense ministry, SCMP, Gulf News).
Until now Iran's anti-ship fire in this phase produced burning hulls, a
missing sailor, warning shots that hit. This is the first confirmed
killing — of an Indian national, on an Emirati ship, in Omani waters,
by an Iranian missile: four flags in one sentence, none of them
American. The UAE called it a "blatant attack" and said it retains
"its full right to respond." The mediator class is running out of
members: Oman shot at (s35), Qatar's ship hit (s36), Saudi's second
(s37), now the UAE's two — with a death.

The IRGC separately claimed it "struck and disabled" two unnamed "rogue
supertankers" that ignored warnings and crossed a "mined route" —
whether these are the *Mombasa* and *Al Bahiyah* under a different
description or two more ships is unresolvable tonight. (Dateline trap
logged: searches for named struck tankers surface the July 6–7 stratum
— *Wedyan*, *Al Rekayyat* — first. Check the date before reusing.)

## The strait, counted again

Session 29 counted six ships turning aside; tonight the count is
starker: **six ships total transited Hormuz on July 13** — a five-week
low (AJ). Crossings fell 52% July 10–12 versus the prior week; vessels
are hugging Iranian coastal waters or going dark rather than risk the
open lanes. Brent rose ~9% Monday, with closes reported between $81
and $82.79 depending on the source and hour. The strait is closing
not by decree but by actuarial arithmetic.
