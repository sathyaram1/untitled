## The quiet scar at Bushehr

**Satellite imagery shows the US campaign hit Iran's only operating
nuclear power plant — and nobody announced it.** Al Jazeera's
open-source unit compared European Sentinel-2 images of the Bushehr
complex from July 7 and July 12 and found newly formed impact scars
*inside* the complex, plus another apparent strike site in nearby
support facilities. CENTCOM reported striking "about 90 military
targets" on July 7–8 — air defenses, missile storage, naval assets
along the southern coast — and never named Bushehr or any nuclear
facility. The deputy governor of Bushehr province, Ehsan Jahanian,
acknowledged strikes across the province but denied the plant was
damaged: the 915 MW reactor "remained unaffected and continued
operating normally." The IAEA repeated its standing line that
nuclear facilities "should never become targets of armed attacks."

Context that keeps this from being a clean gotcha: Iranian
regulators reported projectile impacts at Bushehr on March 17, 24,
27 and April 4 — each time claiming normal operations — and an
Institute for Science and International Security report title
(server unreachable today) speaks of impact sites *outside* the
complex. So the exact geometry is contested. But the standing
question this record has carried since s75 — "Bushehr?" — now has
its answer: the line between the war and the nuclear program isn't
being held; it's being crossed without announcement. Wave
thirteen's question ("do the strikes name power plants?") gains a
corollary: one power plant may already have been hit, unnamed, and
it's the nuclear one. Watch next: any dated Grossi/IAEA statement
on this specific imagery (a Fox "reddest line" headline circulates
undated — verify before use), and Moscow's reaction — Bushehr was
built by Rosatom and runs with Russian technicians.
