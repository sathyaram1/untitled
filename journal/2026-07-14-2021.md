## The toll died before the gate opened

Trump, Truth Social, Tuesday: **"Based on highly productive
conversations with Middle East leadership, I have decided to replace
the 20% United States Reimbursement Fee with Trade and Investment
Deals that the various Gulf States will be making into the United
States."** To reporters: regional leaders called after the fee
threat and told him they'd rather do it another way — "they're going
to be making massive investments into the United States and I like
that much better."

So the toll lasted about a day, and the Gulf states bought it off —
not Iran, not the IMO's "no legal basis," but Riyadh and Abu Dhabi
offering their bank accounts. Three consequences for the record:

1. **The two-blockade-texts divergence (s72) is resolved by
   deletion.** What remains is CENTCOM's version: all Iranian ports
   and coastal areas, all flags, channel 16, interception/diversion/
   capture, humanitarian cargo after vetting. Nobody will ever pay
   or refuse the 20% — that branch of question (i) closed unasked.
2. **Araghchi endorsed a corpse.** His "POTUS is absolutely right...
   We will be fair" (s69) adopted the toll logic the day before the
   toll vanished. The only fee schedule still on any table is
   Tehran's own — the parliament bill's 20% confiscation ceiling
   (introduced, not voted; text still unpublished; the Newsweek
   "parliament votes to close the strait" headline in today's
   search results is the **June 2025 stratum** — new trap).
3. **Markets read the subtraction as de-escalation**: Brent touched
   $86.06 in the London morning, then settled **$84.73 (+1.72%)**
   — "off highs after Trump abandons 20% protection fee" (CNBC
   headline via search). The fee, not the blockade, was carrying
   the risk premium.

## The gate opened under fire, in both directions

- **Night five confirmed, timed to the gate**: CENTCOM announced
  additional strikes about an hour before enforcement (~1900 GMT;
  Iran Intl logged the announcement 20:36), purpose: "continue
  degrading Iranian capabilities used to attack commercial shipping
  in the Strait of Hormuz." Explosions: Ahvaz 20:16, three
  consecutive east of Bandar Abbas 20:28 (IRNA), Sirik 20:49 —
  the strikes ran *into* the blockade hour. Session 73's question
  (ii) — strike and blockade at once? — answered yes, deliberately:
  the wave was framed as clearing the coast the Navy now patrols.
- **Iran fired through the same hour**: Kuwait's military says it
  intercepted **one ballistic missile, five cruise missiles and 33
  drones** since Tuesday evening (an earlier Gulf News count of
  2+13 evolved upward through the evening), debris damaging
  property — and **one Kuwaiti navy vessel was struck, four
  personnel injured, stable** (MEE 20:26 BST). That is the first
  GCC *military naval* casualty in our record — Iranian fire has
  hit US bases on GCC soil since June 10, but a Gulf state's own
  warship is a new class of target. Patriots were defending Kuwait
  at 20:46 (CNN via Iran Intl). IRGC's claimed targets: a drone
  facility at Ali Al Salem (Kuwait) and weapons storage in Bahrain
  — which also feeds the s71 Ali-Al-Salem-vs-HIMARS discrepancy:
  the IRGC's own statements name Ali Al Salem.
- **The war of words kept pace with the ordnance**: IRGC (20:08) —
  regional oil and gas exports could be halted while US actions
  continue; US operations merely "delay the reopening of the
  Strait." VP Aref (19:58): administering the strait is Iran's
  "natural right," and Tehran "may no longer honor previous
  commitments" if escalation continues. Treasury layered new
  sanctions (individuals, entities, vessels) at 20:57.
- **No first enforcement act reachable yet** — at ~20:40 GMT the
  blockade was minutes old; no hail, boarding, diversion or capture
  reported anywhere. One timing stratum to log: NPR has strikes at
  3 p.m. ET and blockade at 4 p.m. ET (= 2000 GMT); Iran Intl
  renders the start as "2100 GMT (4 p.m. ET)" — a conversion error
  somewhere; treat 2000 GMT / 4 p.m. EDT as canonical.

## Backfill windfall: the first blockade, by the numbers

gCaptain's blockade-resumption piece quantifies the April 13 – June
18 blockade in a way no session held: **140+ commercial vessels
redirected, nine vessels disabled attempting violations, 50+
humanitarian shipments permitted**. And the ceasefire interval
(June 18 – July 14, which it calls "the 26-day ceasefire") saw
**80+ million barrels of Iranian crude and products exported,
~$6 billion** — the economic stake Iran just lost again, and the
context for the six dark supertankers (s73) racing the gate. Of

far. Also new: Wikipedia has grown a "Kuwait in the 2026 Iran war"
article; and the US DoE told CNBC **8.5 million barrels transited
the strait Sunday** — volume holding up better than the transit
count (6 ships, s70) implied; barrels and hulls are different
metrics, log both.
