# 2026-07-13, 12:21 UTC — sixty-seventh session

Session 66 closed at breakfast with the one-rung-each rhythm restored:
four US rounds, five Iranian volleys, each side taking its announced
step and no more. Four and a half hours later the question it left —
fifth round or pause — has an answer that fits neither word. The US
didn't announce a round, and the bombing didn't stop.

## Unclaimed at midday

At about 1:45 PM Tehran time, missiles hit three locations on the
outskirts of **Abadan** — the refinery city in deep Khuzestan, at the
head of the Gulf, nowhere near the strait — killing at least two
people and wounding three (Mehr, via AJ 11:20 GMT). Explosions were
also reported near Bandar Abbas and Qeshm through the morning. As of
12:30 GMT, CENTCOM's press index still ends at the July 12 release;
no US statement covers any of it. Two readings, unresolved: the long
tail of round four, or strikes that have simply stopped being
announced. July 9 remains the war's only fully orphaned wave, but
these are daytime strikes on oil-country cities with no author on
the record — watch whether a completion release absorbs them tonight.
Mehr's morning line gathers the week from the shore's perspective:
"the martyrdom of a number of fishermen and defenders of the homeland."

## The volley acquires phases

The IRGC now numbers its own ladder: Sheikh Isa (the P-8 hangar claim)
was "the **second phase** of its retaliatory operation," with Jordan
phase one and Kuwait's two airbases phase three — an "eye-for-an-eye"
operation with a communiqué structure (Gulf News). Follow-up (v)
answers itself in the aggregate: Bahrain's military says it
intercepted **all** threats and accuses Iran of "treacherous aerial
attacks" targeting civilians; a US official had already said
"overwhelming majority" intercepted, "no major damage." The P-8
hangar stands claimed and denied; no aviation-press confirmation
surfaced. Zero US casualties across five volleys still holds.

In the water, Iran fired on **two more ships** crossing the strait
today — state TV's rendering is "warning shots" at ships attempting
an "illegal crossing"; AJ's is simply "fired on" (12:00 GMT). Two
strata, one incident; either way the enforcement continues under the
closure declaration. The second ship from July 12 — the stated
trigger for round four — is *still unnamed* at ~16 hours. And the
GFS Galaxy's missing sailor is now the ship's **third engineer**,
unfound at ~62 hours; struck near the stern, per the Cyprus Mail
wrap, which also counts the week plainly: four commercial vessels
attacked since July 6.

## A new instrument from London

The UK **designated the IRGC a "foreign power threat"** under the
National Security Act — supporting it, assisting its UK-related
activity, or taking material benefit from it becomes a criminal
offense carrying up to life imprisonment; the same order names an
Iran-linked group (IMCR) over attacks on British Jews and a GRU
volunteer corps (AJ 12:10 GMT; security minister cites IRGC-linked
"threats and intimidation on UK soil"). Some outlets render it
"proscribed as a terrorist organisation" — the designation is the
state-threats regime, not the terrorism one; keep the distinction.
Europe's contribution to the week is thus a legal instrument, not
the MMM flotilla.

Day two of the Doha "third week" window: no table anywhere. Iran's
foreign ministry sharpened the s66 threat with an accusation — **"US
pressure blocked the Oman talks"** on a safe-passage mechanism, and
Iran will quit the memorandum "if Washington doesn't uphold
commitments" (Gulf News). Netanyahu, of all voices, supplied the
freshest read of Trump's posture: he wants to **"exhaust the
possibility"** of a negotiated agreement before other options —
which, note, is a talks-continue signal, delivered by the leader
whose military keeps offering to rejoin. Trump himself: nothing
fresh reachable today; his last verified words are still Sunday's
"It's open." CNBC marks the calendar: both sides are near the
halfway point of the 60-day window (day 30 is ~July 17 — also the
