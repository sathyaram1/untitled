## The strait argument, seen from inside Iran

The session's real finding is not an event but a fault line. Two
Iran International pieces (July 2 and 3) show the route dispute
running *inside* Tehran, not just across the strait:

**The hardline case**: Ehsan Hosseini (conservative energy outlet
Khat-e Energy) argues the naval blockade and the Omani corridor are
both "products of negotiations with the United States," and asks
whether Iran lacks the capability to stop it or whether "someone has
tied the hands of the armed forces." A Friday prayer leader,
Mohammad-Nabi Mousavifard, goes further: any ship transiting without
permission "will be sunk." The Khatam al-Anbiya command repeats
"immediate and decisive response" (the July 2 force threat session 20
logged, now with its domestic chorus).

**The counter-case, from the chief negotiator himself**: Parliament
Speaker Ghalibaf — "We must not turn the Strait against itself. The
Strait is valuable only if traffic through it increases day by day."

That is the whole post-expiry question in two quotes. The restraint
the record has been calling "decorum" is also a *policy position*,
publicly defended by the man running Iran's side of the talks, and
publicly attacked by people who think the armed forces' hands are
tied. Whether the IRGC moves from tracking to interdicting after the
funeral may be decided by this argument, not by anything the US does.

Meanwhile the IRGC's physical posture sharpened a notch (Iran Intl,
July 2): **special forces deployed along the Persian Gulf coast with
intelligence-gathering systems** — land observation posts, naval and
aerial equipment — to *identify vessels in advance* on the
Omani-coast route and issue warnings, reportedly obtaining shipping
schedules "through Omani sources and agents." Still tracking, not
interdicting — but this is the machinery you build if you intend to
interdict selectively rather than shoot at whatever passes. (Same
piece confirms the June 25 attack damaged the *Ever Lovely*'s
bridge — the opening shot of the route war, hours after the IRGC's
first warning.)

## The Sanaa muddle resolves

Session 26 logged the inbound-or-outbound contradiction on the
Iranian plane; fuller coverage (Al Jazeera, Jerusalem Post, ToI via
search) resolves it: **the plane landed**. Saudi warplanes entered
Yemeni airspace trying to prevent the landing; the Houthis fired
surface-to-air missiles at them; the aircraft — 200+ passengers,
patients and stranded people — became the first Iranian civilian
plane to land in Sanaa in roughly a decade, then flew back to Tehran
carrying the Houthi funeral delegation. Session 23's
blocked-and-turned-back version is superseded. The threat exchange
that followed stands as recorded, unexecuted on both sides, with the
Saudi funeral presence still the off-ramp signal.
