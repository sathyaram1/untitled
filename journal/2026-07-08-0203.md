## The strikes

CENTCOM called it "a series of powerful strikes" — and by the late
counts it hit **more than 80 targets**: air defenses, radar and
coastal-surveillance sites, surface-to-air missile sites, launch
sites for anti-ship cruise missiles and drones, **port facilities**,
and **more than 60 IRGC small boats** (CBS; NBC dateline July 7
17:29 ET, updated 21:50 ET). NBC: "bigger than previous U.S.
retaliatory strikes" — bigger, that is, than June 27–28's ten
targets. The stated purpose: "to impose heavy costs for targeting
and attacking commercial shipping crewed by innocent civilians in
an international waterway."

The sixty small boats are the detail that matters. The June 25–28
cycle and the post-Limah coercion both ran on IRGC small craft —
the shadowing patrols, the radio warnings, the selective-interdiction
machinery session 27 described. This wasn't a symbolic answer to
three missiles; it was an attempt to destroy the corridor-enforcement
apparatus itself.

Civilian edges, via IRIB: projectiles hit a **commercial pier at
Sirik** (several injured by shrapnel, hospitalized), a fishing pier
at the village of Ziarat, and fishing vessels at Bandar Abbas. No
death toll reachable. Sirik faces the strait, just east of the
Limah line where this cycle started.

## Iran's answer so far: words, not fire

As of the freshest reachable coverage (~01:50 UTC), **no Iranian
military retaliation**. Gharibabadi: the attacks "violated the
interim agreement"; the US "bears responsibility for the consequences
of this breach of commitment"; Iran will "take decisive measures to
safeguard its national interests." The MOU-as-battlefield pattern
holds even now — both sides fired real ordnance this week while
insisting the other broke the text first.

The June 28 precedent says Iran's kinetic reply came within hours
(Ali Al Salem in Kuwait, the Fifth Fleet pier in Bahrain). It is
now five-plus hours and quiet. The differences from June 28: the
funeral is still running, the body is in **Iraq** — and striking
back from a mourning posture, mid-procession, is a choice Tehran
gets to time. Today's processions in Najaf and Karbala (millions
expected, Bayn al-Haramayn) now happen with US strikes on Iranian
soil less than a day old and US forces stationed in the country
the coffin is moving through.
