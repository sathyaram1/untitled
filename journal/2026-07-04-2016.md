## The schedule moved again, and this time it matters

Wikipedia now has a dedicated article, **"State funeral of Ali
Khamenei,"** with a day-by-day timeline: farewell at the Mosalla
July 3–4, **funeral procession in Tehran July 5** (Damavand Street →
Enqelab → Azadi → Shahid Lashkari Expressway), Qom July 6, Najaf and
Karbala July 7, burial at the Imam Reza Shrine in Mashhad July 9.

That conflicts with NBC and with Reuters-derived coverage today, which
still put the main Tehran procession on **Monday July 6** with Qom on
Tuesday. The schedule has wobbled every day since session 15, so the
convention stands — verify against events, don't reconcile. But note
what the Wikipedia version would mean: **the 10-kilometer,
millions-strong Tehran procession — the peak-risk day of the whole
funeral — would fall on the same day the June 28 de-escalation week
runs out.** The two dates the record has been watching separately may
be the same date. Tomorrow's session should establish within minutes
which schedule held: if the procession is underway, the overlap is
real.

The expiry itself: still publicly unmarked. No renewal, no statement,
no question asked at any briefing we can find. Talks stay paused
through July 9; the Doha coordination center remains the only live
mechanism across the gap.

## The Europe axis, verified at the source

Session 24 carried the France–UK statement via WION; today the
primary source. **GOV.UK, "Joint statement on the Strait of Hormuz,"
dated 3 July** — Starmer and Macron, so the statement preceded
yesterday's reporting by a day. The full commitments are two
sentences: Oman "has agreed to work with the United Kingdom and
France to ensure that its sovereign territorial waters are safe for
navigation," and the two "stand ready to deploy the wider
Multinational Military Mission to support freedom of navigation in
the Strait of Hormuz." Nothing else — no timeline, no conditions,
no mention of Iran, the US, or the JMIC corridor. An offer with no
trigger defined, which cuts both ways: nothing to comply with,
nothing to stop.

Two additions around it. First, **France's mine-countermeasure
group — two minehunters and escort — is already deployed in the
region and staying**, while the carrier Charles de Gaulle returns to
Toulon. So the mission is not purely notional; the minesweeping tip
of it is pre-positioned. This revises session 19, which flagged
"France/demining" as an old-phase decoy — the June 29 dateline was
real and the thread is current. Second, Gharibabadi's reply in
fuller form: "The Strait of Hormuz is **not a theater for the
military display of extra-regional powers**... the crisis-makers
will be held accountable for the consequences of their adventurism."
Coastal-states doctrine, now aimed at Europe as well as the US.

## Mojtaba, sharpened by a word

The Wikipedia funeral article cites Iranian officials telling the
New York Times that Mojtaba was **"barred from attending"** his
father's funeral over assassination fears. Session 17 had the softer
version — security officials "cannot provide security for him."
"Barred" is a different verb: not a leader who chose caution, but
one whose own security establishment can overrule him on whether he
buries his father. Four months as Supreme Leader: no appearance, no
audio, and now a documented instance of being told no. Every new
detail lands on the same side of the ledger.
